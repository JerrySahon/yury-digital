Our idea

A soft band. A little friend on top. Serious sensing inside.

Many children pull a medical sensor off. A friend is different. The friend sits on a band, the band reads the body, and the parent gets a quiet message on the phone. No screen on the child and no alarm in the room.

  • Heart rate and HRV
  • Skin conductance, EDA
  • Skin temperature
  • 3-axis motion
  • Five-day battery
  • No screen, ever

The sensor pod snaps out. The friend snaps on. The same pod works in all three bands. In a 2025 survey of 40 parents of nonverbal children, 67.5% supported a wearable stress detector (Weber et al.).

What it is based on

The body changes first. That can be measured.

Four studies from 2019 to 2026 use a wearable sensor to catch an episode. Three of them predict it, 1 to 10 minutes ahead. The models look for this: heart rate goes up, heart rate variability goes down, and skin conductance bursts come more often.

The four studies

AUROC measures how well a model separates “episode soon” from “all quiet”. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. Models, groups, and horizons differ, so compare with care. In these studies an “episode” means aggression or other challenging behavior, which is not always what a parent calls a meltdown.

Goodwin et al.

2019 · Autism Research

20 young people, ages 6 to 17 · inpatient psychiatric unit · 1 minute ahead

0.51.0

General model 0.71Personal model 0.84

The first study of this kind, and the only one where a personal model clearly won. The group was small, so this result is quoted often but should not stand alone.

Imbiriba et al.

2023 · JAMA Network Open

70 young people, ages 5 to 19 · 4 psychiatric hospitals · 3 minutes ahead

0.51.0

Main result 0.80Best case 0.87

  • The largest study: 429 sessions, 497 hours, 6,665 episodes. 86 enrolled, 70 analyzed. 8 refused the sensor, 8 were discharged.
  • 0.80 is the main result, logistic regression at 3 minutes. 0.87 is the best case, SVM on a split by session.
  • On people the model had never seen: 0.82 (SVM) and 0.77 (logistic regression).
  • Here the personal model did worse: 0.74.

Kartha et al.

2026 · preprint

9 males, ages 10 to 21 · special-ed class · 10 minutes ahead

0.51.0

At 10 minutes 0.78 ± 0.10

  • The only study outside a clinic: 110.7 hours in a classroom, with the sensor on the ankle for 8 of 9 children.
  • Still above 0.75 at 30 minutes.
  • Precision 0.31, recall 0.55. That means about 2 of 3 alerts are false. It is the only study that gives this number.

Bahrami Rad et al.

2025 · Physiological Measurement

11 boys, ages 10 to 20 · The Center for Discovery · detection, not ahead

0.51.0

Accelerometer 0.71

This study detects an episode as it happens, it does not warn ahead. An uncomfortable result worth saying out loud: the best channel here was motion, not skin conductance. Personal models did not win consistently. A cheap accelerometer should not be written off.

This group likely overlaps with Kartha 2026, so we do not add these 11 children to the total.

What we took from them

A personal model helps, but not always.

Better in 2019, worse in 2023. The pilot tests it directly: a general model plus two weeks of personal calibration.

Motion is a strong channel.

In one study an accelerometer alone beat skin conductance at detecting episodes (AUROC 0.71). So even a simple module carries signal.

The horizon can reach 10 minutes.

On the ankle, in a classroom. The sensor went there because children keep it on, and that matters as much as the model.

The hard part: about 2 of 3 alerts were false.

A parent who gets pulled away for nothing stops listening. So we do not start with alerts. We start silent: record, label, and look back. “Yesterday at 16:40 it began eight minutes before you noticed” has no false alarms at all. Live alerts come later, and only if the numbers hold.

Where to measure

The wrist is easy, but not the best place for skin conductance.

Sweat glands are denser on the hands and feet. In a lab comparison of 45 students, the wrist responded least of all sites. So the same pod moves: wrist, arm, ankle. The pilot chooses the place by data, and by what the child will actually keep on.

The gap

99 children in the literature. None of them at home.

Every model was trained in a hospital, a clinic, or a classroom. Home is where a parent needs the warning most, and we found no labeled data from there. That is the piece Emozu wants to collect first.

Method

The unit of data is a labeled episode, not an hour of recording.

A target of about 15 episodes per child, which at home means 6 to 8 weeks. The parent marks an episode with one tap. Every “what helped” mark is one more label.

  1. Wears the friendAt home, all day
  2. Signal is recordedHR, EDA, temperature, motion
  3. Parent marks itOne tap in the bot
  4. Window is cutThe minutes before
  5. Two modelsGeneral, plus 2 weeks of personal calibration
  6. Parent is warnedMinutes ahead, phase B only

“What helped” goes back into the data. The loop teaches the model this child.

What we measure

At 3, 5, and 10 minutes ahead:

  • AUROC
  • Sensitivity
  • PPV
  • False alarms per day

In phase B we compare how often and how long episodes last on “on” days and “off” days.

A Telegram labeling bot is built and being tried out.
